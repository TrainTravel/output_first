# ADHD evidence brief — what researchers actually recommend

> Compiled from additudemag.com articles, cross-verified against Google Scholar.
> Last verified: 2026-06-02. Source: additudemag.com + scholar.google.com.

## Methodology

Researchers were sourced by surveying additudemag.com for ADHD content authored by, or prominently quoting, named experts with PhD/MD/PsyD credentials, then cross-checked on Google Scholar (PubMed where Scholar was thin) for **multiple peer-reviewed ADHD publications** — not just books or blog posts. For each verified researcher, at least one specific paper title and year is noted. Researchers whose record was primarily commercial (books, paid courses, magazine columns without a research footprint) were excluded — see "Notable exclusions." Effect sizes are quoted only where supplied by the article or original paper; otherwise the evidence-strength label carries the weight. Pharmacology and dosing are deliberately omitted.

## Verified researchers cited in this brief

- **Russell A. Barkley, PhD** — Clinical psychologist; 280+ scientific articles. Foundational work on DESR. Rep paper: Barkley & Fischer, 2010, *J Psychopathol Behav Assess* (emotional impulsiveness in adult ADHD).
- **Joel T. Nigg, PhD** — Professor of Psychiatry, Oregon Health & Science University; directs OHSU ADHD Research Program. ~200 peer-reviewed papers. Rep paper: *"Toward a revised nosology for ADHD heterogeneity"* (Nigg et al., 2020, *Biol Psychiatry CNNI*).
- **Stephen P. Hinshaw, PhD** — Distinguished Professor of Psychology, UC Berkeley; Vice Chair of Psychiatry, UCSF. h-index ~134; 420+ articles. Rep paper: *"Annual Research Review: ADHD in girls and women"* (Hinshaw et al., 2022, *J Child Psychol Psychiatry*).
- **Thomas E. Brown, PhD** (d. 2022) — Yale clinical psychologist; 30+ peer-reviewed papers; developed the Brown Executive Function/Attention Scales. Rep paper: Brown, 2008, *Current Attention Disorders Reports*.
- **John J. Ratey, MD** — Associate Clinical Professor of Psychiatry, Harvard Medical School; 60+ peer-reviewed articles on exercise neurobiology and ADHD.
- **Lidia Zylowska, MD** — Associate Professor of Psychiatry, University of Minnesota; cofounder, UCLA MARC. Rep paper: *"Mindfulness Meditation Training in Adults and Adolescents With ADHD: A Feasibility Study"* (Zylowska et al., 2008, *J Atten Disord*).
- **J. Russell Ramsay, PhD, ABPP** — Formerly co-director, Penn Adult ADHD Treatment & Research Program; editorial board, *J Atten Disord*. Rep: *Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy for Adult ADHD* (Ramsay & Rostain, 2015) + associated CBT trials.
- **Sandra Kooij, MD, PhD** — Professor of Adult ADHD, Amsterdam UMC / PsyQ. Rep papers: Van Veen, Kooij et al., 2010, *Biol Psychiatry* (delayed circadian rhythm in adult ADHD); van Andel, Bijlenga, Kooij et al., 2022, *J Biol Rhythms* (chronotherapy RCT).
- **Jamie M. Zeitzer, PhD** — Professor of Psychiatry and Behavioral Sciences, Stanford. Rep paper: *"Temporal integration of light flashes by the human circadian system"* (Zeitzer et al., 2014, *J Clin Invest*).
- **Sam Goldstein, PhD** — Editor-in-Chief, *Journal of Attention Disorders*; clinical neuropsychologist, University of Utah School of Medicine.
- **Mark Bertin, MD** — Developmental pediatrician; faculty, New York Medical College; APSARD board. Practice-focused; lighter peer-reviewed record — included with that caveat.
- **James W. Pennebaker, PhD** — Professor Emeritus of Psychology, UT Austin. Not an ADHD researcher per se, but his expressive-writing paradigm is what additude cites when recommending journaling. Rep paper: *"Expressive Writing in Psychological Science"* (Pennebaker, 2018, *Perspectives on Psychological Science*); 200+ studies validate the protocol.

## 1. Treatments

**Cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT) for adult ADHD**
- Skills-based therapy targeting procrastination, time management, self-criticism cycles, and the emotional fallout of chronic underperformance. Scaffolding, not cure.
- Backed by: Ramsay (Penn); meta-analyses summarized in Ramsay & Rostain (2015).
- Evidence: `Meta-analysis / RCT-supported` — modest-to-moderate effects on functional outcomes; smaller on core symptoms.
- OutputFirst relevance: directly aligned. Thought capture, restructuring of automatic thoughts, self-compassion scripting. The app should be CBT-adjacent, not a CBT replacement.

**Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT) adapted for adult ADHD**
- The Hesslinger protocol: 12–14 week skills-only group (psychoeducation + mindfulness + emotion regulation + distress tolerance + interpersonal effectiveness).
- Backed by: Hesslinger et al. (2002, *Eur Arch Psychiatry Clin Neurosci*); Philipsen et al. (2007); recent multicenter trials.
- Evidence: `Multiple studies` — preliminary efficacy for emotional dysregulation and comorbid depression; less established than CBT.
- OutputFirst relevance: high. The "name the emotion → tolerate the distress → act effectively" loop is exactly what a journaling flow can rehearse.

**Behavioral parent training (for kids)**
- Antecedent-behavior-consequence coaching, praise schedules, environmental restructuring.
- Backed by: MTA Study (1999); Daley et al. and Coates et al. meta-analyses.
- Evidence: `Meta-analysis / RCT-supported` — strongest non-pharmacological base for childhood ADHD; weaker on blinded symptom ratings.
- OutputFirst relevance: out of scope. Note for future: a "parent reflection" mode could route here.

**Mindfulness-based interventions (Zylowska MAPs)**
- 8-week program; shorter sit times than MBSR; ADHD-specific psychoeducation, attention training, self-compassion.
- Backed by: Zylowska et al. (2008, *J Atten Disord*) + subsequent RCTs.
- Evidence: `Multiple studies` — small RCTs show improvements in attention, executive function, emotion regulation.
- OutputFirst relevance: high. The Breathe step aligns; "shorter sits, ADHD-adapted" is worth naming as a design principle.

## 2. Nutrients & supplements

**Omega-3 (EPA/DHA)**
- Most-studied supplement for ADHD. Meta-analyses show small but statistically significant symptom improvement; effect sizes ~0.16–0.20 SMD (well below stimulants, but real).
- Backed by: Bloch & Qawasmi (2011, *J Am Acad Child Adolesc Psychiatry*); Nigg endorses dietary fish or supplementation as a "low-effort" strategy.
- Evidence: `Meta-analysis / RCT-supported` — small effect.
- OutputFirst relevance: out of scope. App should not recommend dosages.

**Iron (only if deficient — screen first)**
- Low serum ferritin correlates with ADHD severity in children. Supplementation helps deficient kids; harmful otherwise.
- Backed by: Konofal et al. (2008, *Pediatr Neurol*); Nigg flags as "high-effort," requires blood test.
- Evidence: `Multiple studies` — conditional on deficiency.
- OutputFirst relevance: out of scope. Never recommend iron.

**Zinc**
- Lower zinc correlates with severity in some populations; supplementation as stimulant adjunct has weak support (Akhondzadeh et al., 2004).
- Evidence: `Single study / Expert consensus, limited trials` — recent systematic reviews skeptical.
- OutputFirst relevance: out of scope.

**ADDitude-mentioned, evidence-thin:** Pycnogenol, valerian/lemon balm combinations, multi-ingredient "ADHD support" stacks all have one or two small trials at best. Treat as anecdotal. The app should make no supplement recommendations — full stop.

## 3. Lifestyle

**Exercise — single best-supported lifestyle intervention**
- Aerobic exercise raises dopamine, norepinephrine, BDNF; improves executive function acutely and chronically. EF/attention effect sizes in children with ADHD: ~0.3–0.6 depending on protocol.
- Backed by: Ratey (Harvard); Nigg endorses ~1 hour/day moderate-to-vigorous, cites three meta-analyses where effects "can be extraordinary." Cerrillo-Urbina et al. (2015, *Child Care Health Dev*).
- Evidence: `Meta-analysis / RCT-supported` — robust for acute attention; real but weaker for chronic symptom reduction.
- OutputFirst relevance: medium. A gentle pre-write prompt ("did you move today?") fits without medicalizing.

**Sleep — circadian alignment, not just duration**
- 75–80% of adults with ADHD have delayed sleep phase; melatonin onset ~90 min later than controls. Treatment is chronotherapy: morning light, evening light reduction, clinician-directed timed low-dose melatonin.
- Backed by: Kooij (Amsterdam UMC); van Andel et al. (2022, *J Biol Rhythms*) chronotherapy RCT — 1.5h DLMO advance, 14% symptom reduction. Zeitzer (Stanford) on mechanisms.
- Evidence: `Meta-analysis / RCT-supported` for the link; `Multiple studies` for chronotherapy.
- OutputFirst relevance: medium. Optional daily reminders should default to morning. Don't prescribe melatonin.

**Structure & environment**
- External scaffolding (calendars, checklists, environmental cues) is the executive-function "prosthesis." Barkley and Brown frame this as compensating for the impaired self-management system, not willpower.
- Evidence: `Expert consensus, limited trials` for specific tools; `Meta-analysis / RCT-supported` for the broader principle (encoded in CBT trials).
- OutputFirst relevance: directly aligned. Externalizing cognition (brain dumps, thought garden, to-do triage) is OutputFirst's core proposition.

**Screens and digital media**
- Nigg's group has published on the digital media / ADHD risk relationship. Correlational, modest; not "screens cause ADHD" but heavy passive scroll correlates with attentional symptoms.
- Evidence: `Multiple studies` — directionality debated.
- OutputFirst relevance: low — and the app itself is a screen. Be honest. Mitigation is *active*, low-friction interaction over infinite scroll.

## 4. Emotion regulation — primary section for OutputFirst

**DESR (Deficient Emotional Self-Regulation) as a core ADHD feature**
- Barkley argues — and a growing literature supports — that emotional impulsivity and difficulty modulating emotional reactions are *central* to ADHD, not comorbid. DESR predicts impairment across driving, marriage, employment, and quality of life independent of attention symptoms. Missing from DSM-5 but present across clinical populations.
- Backed by: Barkley; corroborated by Brown's six-cluster model (Activation, Focus, Effort, Emotion, Memory, Action).
- Evidence: `Multiple studies` — theoretical framework with strong observational support.
- OutputFirst relevance: foundational. The app's entire premise — externalize, label, process emotion before acting — directly targets DESR.

**Emotion labeling ("name it to tame it")**
- Putting words on a feeling reduces amygdala reactivity (Lieberman et al., 2007, *Psychol Sci*, "Putting feelings into words"). Endorsed in ADHD contexts by Barkley, Brown, and Susan David (Harvard Medical School).
- Evidence: `Meta-analysis / RCT-supported` in general affective neuroscience.
- OutputFirst relevance: highest priority. The bilingual emotion vocabulary IS an affect-labeling intervention with L2 anchoring as a side effect.

**Expressive writing (Pennebaker protocol)**
- 15–30 min of continuous, unedited writing about an emotionally charged experience, repeated 3–4 days. Validated across 200+ studies; effects on mood, immune markers, behavioral outcomes (e.g., laid-off workers re-employed faster in Spera, Buhrfeind & Pennebaker, 1994).
- Backed by: Pennebaker (UT Austin); ADDitude cites him directly for ADHD journaling.
- Evidence: `Meta-analysis / RCT-supported` broadly; ADHD-specific trials sparse, mechanism applies.
- OutputFirst relevance: highest priority. Write/Free Write are the Pennebaker protocol with ADHD-friendly affordances (rotating prompts, Enter-to-submit, skip always available, short sessions).

**Mindfulness for emotion regulation (Zylowska MAPs)**
- MAPs targets self-regulation of attention, emotions, behavior + self-acceptance + self-compassion. Shorter sits than MBSR.
- Evidence: `Multiple studies`.
- OutputFirst relevance: aligned with Breathe and the non-judgmental tone of reflections.

**Self-compassion**
- Not ADHD-specific but consistently surfaced by Barkley, Bertin, Zylowska as the antidote to the shame loop that compounds DESR. Neff's self-compassion scale is the dominant instrument.
- Evidence: `Multiple studies` general; `Expert consensus, limited trials` in ADHD-specific RCTs.
- OutputFirst relevance: existing self-compassion phrases + CJK translations directly serve this.

**Gratitude practice**
- Three-good-things and gratitude letters: Emmons & McCullough (2003, *J Pers Soc Psychol*). ADHD-specific research thinner; rationale is shifting negativity bias.
- Evidence: `Multiple studies` general; `Anecdotal / mechanism only` ADHD-specific.
- OutputFirst relevance: directly served by the Gratitude step.

## Notable exclusions

**Researchers in additude but not verified on Scholar (or primarily commercial output):**
- **Edward M. Hallowell, MD** — highly visible; co-author of *Driven to Distraction* and *ADHD 2.0*. PubMed shows few first-author empirical papers; primarily clinician-author-popularizer. His recommendations track verified researchers, but his specific dose claims shouldn't carry Nigg/Barkley weight.
- **Ari Tuckman, PsyD** — author and clinician; broad practice, light peer-reviewed footprint. Useful clinical synthesis, not primary evidence.
- **Caroline Buzanko, PhD** — additude article author on child emotion regulation; no substantive peer-reviewed ADHD record on Scholar.
- **Sandy / Sanford Newmark, MD** — UCSF integrative pediatrics; supplement protocol advocate. Light peer-reviewed record; treat specific supplement endorsements with skepticism.
- **Shawn Achor** — Harvard-trained positive-psychology popularizer attached to additude's gratitude/reactivity claim. Not an ADHD researcher.

**Hyped treatments evidence does not support:**
- **Neurofeedback / EEG biofeedback** — Cortese et al. (2016, *JAACAP*) and Sonuga-Barke et al. (EAGG) meta-analyses find blinded ratings show null-to-small effects. Unblinded parent ratings show improvement — that's placebo/expectancy. Still marketed as evidence-based; it isn't.
- **"Brain training" apps (Cogmed, Lumosity-style)** — Near-transfer (better at the trained task), no far-transfer to ADHD symptoms in blinded trials. Sonuga-Barke meta-analyses.
- **Most ADHD supplement stacks** — Pycnogenol, ginkgo, valerian, "focus" blends. One or two small trials at best. Omega-3 and conditional iron are exceptions.
- **Restrictive elimination diets without supervision** — Nigg flags as high-effort and deficiency-risking; clinician-only.

## Implications for OutputFirst (≤ 300 words)

**Already supported well:**
- *Affect labeling* — bilingual emotion vocabulary IS affect labeling with L2 anchoring as a side effect (Lieberman; Barkley).
- *Expressive writing* — Write / Free Write are Pennebaker protocol in ADHD-friendly chunks. 200+ study literature applies.
- *Externalized executive function* — brain dump, thought garden, to-do triage compensate for the impaired self-management system (Barkley, Brown).
- *Self-compassion + gratitude* — peer-reviewed bases (Neff; Emmons & McCullough), well served by existing steps.
- *Mindfulness with short sit times* — Zylowska MAPs principle; Breathe step aligns.

**Worth surfacing more deliberately (without going clinical):**
- A one-line "why this works" near Emotions — affect labeling reduces amygdala reactivity (Lieberman 2007). Educates without lecturing.
- Optional daily reminder defaults to morning, not late evening — Kooij circadian work.
- Breathe / sit-time defaults are short by design — Zylowska principle, worth naming in onboarding.

**Explicitly NOT:**
- Recommend specific supplements, doses, or brands. Liability and bad science.
- Recommend sleep medications including melatonin. Out of scope.
- Claim to "treat" ADHD. It supports researcher-endorsed practices; different claim.
- Adopt neurofeedback or brain-training framings. Evidence does not support.
- Become a parenting platform — different evidence base, scope creep.
